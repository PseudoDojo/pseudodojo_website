What are pseudopotentials?

Pseudopotentials (or 'pseudos' for short) are effective potentials describing isolated atoms or ions. They are used to simplify electronic structure calculations of material systems by replacing the nuclear potential with an effective potential consisting of the nucleus screened by a set of core electrons. This allows the simulation to approximately simulate electronic structure behavior using only a restricted set of chemically relevant valence electrons. For more on the mathematics, we recommend this Abinit documentation page. For a more qualitative introduction, we recommend you start with the Wikipedia page on pseudopotentials.

What are PAW datasets?

Projector augmented wave (PAW) datasets function like pseudopotentials for electronic structure suites with a built-in PAW formalism, but the information contained in the dataset files differs from that of a norm-conserving pseudos. A variety of publicly available programs exist to generate these datasets, such as atompaw or the Vanderbilt USPP code. PAW dataset generators construct a local ionic pseudopotential using the chosen PAW basis functions via a method that is closely analogous to Vanderbilt's ultrasoft pseudopotential generation. For a given atom, a PAW dataset typically includes

  • the radial grid on which all ensuing functions are defined,
  • the all-electron (AE) wavefunctions, the pseudo (PS) wavefunctions, and the projector functions for all valence orbitals,
  • the AE core charge density,
  • the PS core charge density,
  • the PS valence charge density,
  • a local ionic pseudopotential, and
  • information for the construction of the compensation charge density.

We recommend this pedagogical introductory video to understand more about the PAW method and the functions involved.

Z, in the top left, is the element's atomic number. The number # in the upper right corner of the element boxes (corresponding to "nv" in the .djrepo files) indicates the number of valence electronic orbitals treated explicitly as part of the valence (i.e., the set of outermost orbitals not included in the frozen core). Take Carbon, for example, which has electrons in the 1s, 2s, and 2p orbitals. Pseudopotentials might only explicitly consider the electrons in the 2s and 2p shells. So for Carbon, nv = 2.

The ecut hints correspond to our recommendations for the cutoff energy in Hartree. The higher the value, the more accurate the calculation will be, but also the more computationally heavy. The exact meaning of the hints can be found in the original PseudoDojo paper. As a rule of thumb, the low suggestion is good for a quick calculation or as a starting point for convergence studies. The middle cutoff is both accurate and computationally light enough for high-throughput calculations. Beyond the high cutoff energy value, your results are unlikely to change significantly.

What's the difference between standard and stringent accuracy?

We generally generate our pseudopotential tables with two degrees of accuracy in mind. Both levels of accuracy provide one pseudopotential per element, each of which fulfills the precision criteria of our tests (see corresponding papers for details). However, around half the elements of the stringent table contain different, more accurate pseudopotentials than their counterparts in the standard table. These stringent pseudos are usually accompanied by higher cutoff energy ecut suggestions, and they sometimes incorporate more valence orbitals than the standard ones, although the specific differences in variables considered at generation depend on the characteristics of the specific element. For details on how each table differentiates its standard and stringent accuracies, we recommend you read the paper(s) corresponding to that table.

As always, though, the standard tables feature pseudopotentials that we consider to be a good compromise between computational expense and chemical accuracy.

How do I cite the PseudoDojo?

The web interface has a built-in citation function. The citation box, which looks something like

PLEASE CITE
van Setten et al. (2018) and Hamann (2013) and Jing et al. (2026) and Perdew et al. (1996).

gives you a list of citations that updates dynamically with your pseudopotential selection criteria. Click anywhere on the box itself for the citation list in Chicago style to be copied automatically to your clipboard. Alternatively, click on the BibTeX button to download the list of citations in BibTeX format.

Is there a way to make slight modifications to these pseudos for my own purposes?

In general, the pseudos you find here are ready to use. If you have specifications you'd like to change, you are also free to use the inputs we provide as a starting point to generate your own pseudopotentials. For ONCVPSP pseudos, you'll need to download ONCVPSP. For JTH PAW datasets, download atompaw

Do you also have the pseudo for my favorite GGA or LDA functional?

We provide PBE, PBEsol, and sometimes LDA pseudopotentials. To generate pseudopotentials with other exchange-correlation functionals, you can use our input files, obtained via the web interface. Using these, you can generate potentials for most LDA and GGA functionals using ONCVPSP and LibXC, which is linked to ONCVPSP and provides many popular and fringe exchange-correlation functionals.

What about hybrids and meta functionals?

These are currently under development, and you're welcome to help us expedite their release. Visit the Contribute page to find out how you can help us make pseudos.

How do I use the input files to generate ONCVPSP potentials without non-linear core correction?

We usually observe better convergence with NLCC, so indeed, all pseudos we provide are generated with it. If you really need one without NLCC, take the input file from the web interface, install ONCVPSP, and before running set the variable icmod to 0 in the input and generate the pseudo.

Do you also have the other elements?

We are currently working on the lanthanides. You're welcome to contribute to pseudopotential development with us. Visit the Contribute page to find out how you can help us make pseudos.

Is it possible to generate NC pseudos in other formats?

ONCVPSP currently supports .psp8 and .upf. Using the psml patch we also generated psml files for use with SIESTA.
